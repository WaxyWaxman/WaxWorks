Before this is built: what M-02 has to say first

The layout needs no decisions. Three tracks, a retractable slab and a sticky right column are how this app already works — E-05 d29 by way of E-02 d38, E-07 d17 and M-01 d17. Folding the two modals into track 3 changes no behaviour: Set status and Re-flag do exactly what d12, d22, d8, d18 already say, in a place that does not cover the table.

Bulk entry needs one decision row, and it is an extension, not a supersession. M-02 d1 says ordering is two-stage — Employees raise pending lines, Managers process them into POs. A line entered here is born placed and never passes through Phase 1. d1 does not forbid that; it never contemplated it. But someone reading d1 alone would conclude the opposite, so it has to be written down in the house pattern — the way d21 clarifies d9 and d22 extends d12. Proposed wording is below. Status: recommended, not yet ratified.

What it is worth. Not paperwork tidiness. d20 makes a receiving scan attach automatically to a matching PO line for that supplier and carry its expected cost and quantity forward. Without these lines on file, a box from a website order arrives as a cold invoice — fully supported (E-02:17), but every line is priced and picked by hand. With them, it receives like any other PO. That is the argument, and it is a receiving argument, not an ordering one.

Proposed rowWording
M-02 d25 An order placed outside the system may be recorded directly as placed lines, without passing through Phase 1. Extends decision 1, which describes how an order leaves through the system and was written before ordering on a supplier's website was a case anyone had to enter afterwards — Their Website is one of M-01's Order via values and Indie Direct Supply is seeded with it. The lines carry the supplier's own reference as the PO number, which d16 already permits (free-text, need not be numeric, and auto-numbering skips it). The separator is not offered: d3's streams are a property of the pending pile, and these lines were never in one. Recording is manager-only — the lexicon defines a PurchaseOrder as a Manager-created reorder. Accepted consequence: nothing verifies that the order was really placed, so a mistyped reference is a PO that will never be received against and will sit on this screen ageing until someone cancels it.
M-02 d26 A recorded-elsewhere order carries the date it was actually placed, not the date it was typed. The follow-up window (d8) runs from that date, the age column and age sort (step 9) read from it, and d19's overdue grouping therefore lands correctly on the first render. Stamping now would make a nine-day-old order look new, which is precisely the failure this screen exists to prevent. Accepted consequence: a line can be overdue the moment it is entered — correct, and the screen should show it that way rather than hiding it for a grace period.
M-02 d27 Recording an order queues the catalogue metadata prefetch, the same as processing a stream does. Step 8 says prefetch is queued on placement; this is a placement. Without it, the one case bulk entry exists to serve — receiving a box against a website order — is also the case that misses locally on every scan and falls back to live lookup (M-02 Inherited, A-12a). Not a new obligation, an explicit reading of step 8 for a placement route it predates.

What each track is for

Left — the order slab (retractable)
Search and scan at the top, then the chips, then the sort, then the open POs. Two controls that do different jobs and look different: chips filter lines (their counts are lines), a PO row scopes the middle to that order (its figure is that order's outstanding lines). Everything on order is a permanent first row, not a state you have to find your way back to — step 9 says this screen lists lines oldest first across everything, and a slab that made you pick a PO first would quietly retire that. Shut, a 52px strip carries the new-order button, an overdue badge, and the POs tiled by their number's tail.
Middle — the lines
The table, essentially as it is, with the overdue group banded rather than silently floated to the top — d19 calls overdue-first a fixed grouping, so it should look like one. Each row is a button. Age is the first column and the biggest thing in the row, because age is what this screen is about.
Right — the line (sticky)
Who is waiting, how old it is, what it is, what has happened to it, and the one thing to do next. Both modals collapse into it.

Why "who is waiting" outranks the age

Same reason Waiting outranks the balance on the Customer card (E-07 d19, E-05 d7) and in-flight outranks A/P on the Supplier card. A late line nobody is waiting on is a supplier problem you get to when you get to it. A late line with a customer's name on it is a phone call you owe a person, and d8 says the follow-up flag is used for exactly that — "to chase the supplier and to warn a waiting customer".

The band is also the tie back to E-07: a customer-attached line's status is read on the Customer's card (M-02 Inherited, E-07 d19). Same fact, two screens; this is the one where you can act on it.

The foot is a ladder

E-07 d20 made the Customer card's primary action a ladder, and M-01 followed it. Here, with a line selected:

  1. Re-flag — the line is overdue. Chasing it is the errand, and d18 makes that a fresh today + n window rather than an extension of a deadline already in the past.
  2. Set status — not overdue. Shipped with their date, Backordered, or Cancelled (d12, d22). The other of the two rides underneath as the secondary.

With no line selected, the foot belongs to the scope instead — Bulk status update and Void this PO for a scoped order, both recorded in M-02's Cancelling and unwinding table and neither of which has a home in the current screen. On Everything, the foot is the new-order button.

Every one of them carries d10's warning where it is read rather than where it is filed: none of this tells the supplier anything.

The log stops being a secret

d23 says every status change is logged on the line — when, what it moved from and to, who did it — because "when did this become backordered" is a question somebody asks a week later. Today that log is inside the Set status modal: to read the history you have to open the thing that changes it, which is the wrong door, and you cannot read it next to the line's figures at all.

In the right track it sits on the floor of the scroll, the same place History sits on the Customer card (E-07 d16) and the Log on the Supplier card (M-01 d4). Receipt writes to the same log (d23), so a part-received line reads as one sequence — which is the only way "2 of 5" ever explains itself.

Why bulk entry is a mode, not a panel

Your instinct to put it on the right was the right question and, I think, the wrong slot. The right track on every screen in this app means one thing: the standing state of whatever is in the middle, and the one action to take on it. An unrelated entry form parked there would be the first time it meant something else — and 344px is a bad place to scan twenty records into.

As a mode, the rule holds in both states. The middle becomes the sheet you are filling; the right becomes that batch's standing — supplier, reference, lines, units, retail, estimated cost against the supplier's minimum — and the foot becomes Place n lines. Nothing about what a track means has to change.

It also answers the thing you were unsure about. Create-new-order does not belong here, and you were right to doubt it: raising a line is Phase 1, it happens from the titlecard where you can see stock and price (E-04, M-02 step 1), and duplicating it here would be a second door to a decided flow. Bulk entry is not that door — it is a different errand that happens to end in the same place.

What the estimated cost is, and is not

The batch figure applies the Supplier's Discount to the sell total, which is what M-02 step 4 says estimated cost is. Two things it does not know, both worth saying on the screen rather than discovering at receiving:

  • It is not what you will be invoiced. Freight, tax and misc stay at Invoice level and are never allocated to items (E-02:100). The estimate is goods only.
  • Discount is the margin field — there is no separate one (M-01 d7 superseded) — so a wrong discount on the Supplier card makes every estimate here wrong in the same direction. Setting it is manager-only (M-01 d11, E-02 d44, A-28a); reading it is not (M-01 d16).

The minimum-order readiness line is shown for the same reason M-02 step 4 shows it on the processing screen — except here it is informational only, because the order has already been placed. Nothing gates on it. A batch below the supplier's minimum is a fact about an order that already exists.

Whose currency each figure is in

The first draft of this mock printed the retail total in the Supplier's currency. That is wrong, and M-01 d14 already says so: "a foreign-currency Supplier shows its own currency; the store equivalent needs the configured rate (M-06) and is not invented." The rule cuts both ways — ours is not to be shown as theirs either.

So: sell price and retail are ours, always CAD, because a sell price is a shelf price. Estimated cost is also CAD, because M-02 step 4 derives it from our retail less their discount — it is a store-currency number that happens to approximate a foreign invoice. Indie Direct Supply will invoice in USD, the screen says so, and nothing here converts. The prototype is already honest about this in Accounts Payable (prototype.md:161); this screen should not be the one that starts inventing rates.

One gap fell out of fixing it. M-01 gives a Supplier's minimum order amount a basis — Net or Retail — but no currency. Indie's minimum of 250 is therefore a bare number, and the mock shows it bare rather than picking a currency for it. Small, and only informational on this screen, but it is a real unstated field on a screen where it does gate (M-02 step 4).

Open, and deliberately left open

  • A reference that collides with one of ours. poNumberTaken exists (AppStore.tsx:687) and the field rejects a duplicate — but d16 only says auto-numbering skips a manually entered number. Whether a collision should be refused outright or merged into the existing PO is not decided, and this mock refuses it without claiming that is the answer.
  • A scan that matches nothing. The record has to exist before a line can point at it. Whether bulk entry can mint a Record inline or must send you to E-04 first is undecided; the mock says so and stops.
  • Customer attachment on a bulk line. d13 creates a Held Sale on receipt of a customer-attached line. Nothing says an entered-after-the-fact line cannot carry one, and it plainly could — but the whole point of this sheet is speed, and a per-line customer picker is the slowest field there is. Left off, and named here rather than quietly dropped.
  • Order acknowledgements (M-02, open) is untouched. Recording an order placed elsewhere is not the same as knowing the supplier has it.
