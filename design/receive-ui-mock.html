What each track is for

Left — the receiving slab (retractable)
What you came here to pick up: drafts in flight, the outstanding PO lines across every open PO, and invoices received lately. Shut, it is a 52px strip carrying the outstanding count and tiles for the invoices in flight. It pushes rather than overlays, the way Find's slab does — you read the worklist against the lines you have already taken in, so an overlay would cover the half you are comparing to.
Middle — the invoice
Fixed head, fixed scan slab, and only the line list scrolls. The scan field is the biggest thing on the track because Phase 2 is what receiving actually is. A resolved scan lands in a staging card under it — cost, list, sell price, grade, qty, live margin — and becomes a line on Enter.
Right — reconcile (sticky)
The till's money track, doing the receiving job. Their paperwork figures are entered here, next to ours, because this is where the two get compared. Finalize is the one accent on the screen.

Moves worth arguing about

  • The reconcile strip moves from the footer to the right track. This contradicts the layout E-02 d37 records. See the box below.
  • The paperwork entry fields move out of the left rail and into the reconcile track, next to the derived figures they are checked against. That is what frees the left track to be purely the worklist.
  • Line entry moves out of the table. Today a new line is the last row of the table; here it is a scan slab plus a staging card above the lines, so entry never scrolls away on a 60-line invoice.
  • The headline figure is the invoice total, not the difference. The difference is the check, but the total is the number staff read aloud and Accounts Payable consumes (M-05). The check sits directly beneath it as a verdict strip so it cannot be read past.
  • Receiving gets deep-linkable selection the way Find has it — /receive/:invoiceId — so the slab can be clicked from anywhere.

The shut strip

Top to bottom: the open chevron in the slot the till rail keeps it in, then + New intake in the accent, Open existing carrying a badge for the drafts in flight, and Search, which opens the slab with the cursor already in the box. Then the three most recent finished sessions.

Search opens the slab too, so the chevron looks redundant until you want the width back without starting an errand. Both exist for the same reason the till rail's chevron does: the bar has to be openable by someone who has not decided what they are opening it for.

Not too busy — on one condition. The copy count is the only second datum on a tile, and it is typographically subordinate: mono, 9.5px, faint, under the short name. The strip still scans as a column of names. It stops working the moment a third datum arrives, so date and total stay in the tooltip.

The badge sits on Open existing rather than on Search, because a draft left half-finished is the thing you can actually lose track of. The outstanding worklist is reference — you go looking for it, it does not go looking for you.

An actual PurchaseOrder
Raising a PO for stock already on the counter inverts M-02 — nothing was ordered, nothing is outstanding, and the worklist would show a line that is complete the instant it exists. I would not do this.

Bare 0910 collides every year and sorts wrong. SH-260910-1 sorts, says what it is, and survives two trade-ins on one afternoon. Tell me which reading you meant before I write the row.

Finalized and paid — try the state buttons

My answer to "hide the scan bar": not on Finalized — on Paid. d4 keeps a finalized invoice editable ("fix a cost, add a line for a carton that turns up late") right up until a manager marks it paid in M-05. Hiding the scan slab at finalize would remove the one control that makes that possible.

So: Draft scans and finalizes. Finalized still scans, with a line under the box saying why it is still open, and the right track's action becomes Save updates. Paid locks — and the slab is replaced, not removed, by a same-height dashed band saying what happened and where amendments go. The track keeps its shape, and the absence explains itself instead of leaving a hole.

Two rows to append

d38 — amends d37. The reconcile figures move from a bottom strip to a sticky right-hand track, making Receiving's frame the till's frame (E-05 d29): worklist, invoice, reconcile. d37's reason stands and is better served — the figures never scroll at any line count, and below 1180px they fall back to d37's own bottom strip.

d39 — amends d31. A second-hand intake with no supplier paperwork mints SH-YYMMDD-n rather than drawing REF#### from a store counter, so the reference says when the stock arrived.

A contradiction I hit on the way

Working out the read-only question turned up two rows in E-02 that do not agree, and it predates this branch:

  • d4 — after finalize, lines and totals stay editable until a manager marks the invoice paid.
  • d31 — an invoice number matching a finalized Invoice opens it read-only.

Phase 3 step 23 sides with d31 ("a finalized invoice is immutable"); d4 is the later thinking and is what M-05 assumes. The layout above implements d4. Worth a resolving row either way, since one of them is telling a future reader the wrong thing.

Still open

  • Push or overlay? Find pushes, the till overlays. The receiving slab pushes. If scanning feels closer to a transaction than a comparison, it should overlay instead.
  • Do drafts belong in the open worklist, or only on the shut strip's badge? "In flight" and "Outstanding" are two kinds of thing sharing one list.
  • Does the slab default shut in Used mode? A second-hand intake has no PO, so the worklist has nothing in it.
