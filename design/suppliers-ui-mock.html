Where the gates ended up

Discount stays manager-only. Locked for an Employee, live for a Manager, who authorizes in place with their own initials and has both names recorded (M-04 d3, d4). This is the outcome that reverses nothing: M-01 d11, E-02 d44 and architecture A-28a all stand as written, no supersession row, no §9 amendment, and d2's "future receiving only" stays safe because the only person who can trip it is the one the review queue would have told anyway.

Employees can see the balances. The one change to the record, and a small one: the lexicon defines manager-only as "an action an Employee cannot perform at all" (lexicon.md:44) — about performing, not viewing. Nothing ever said viewing a balance was gated, so this fills a gap rather than reversing anything. It still needs writing down, because the next person to read "manager-only in its entirety" (M-05 d2) will otherwise conclude the opposite. Record a payment is an A/P action and stays gated — toggle the viewer and watch the foot's secondary button, now the only thing that changes.

One term, worth keeping straight: the gate on Discount is manager-only, never a manager override — that term is retired (M-04 d8, lexicon:41), and reading one for the other is exactly the mistake M-01 d6 made and d11 had to reverse.

What each track is for

Left — the supplier slab (retractable)
Search over name, short code and account # — all three, which M-01 step 2 already says. Chips, a sort control, the list. Shut, a 52px strip carrying New, Search with a result badge, and the cards you have opened, tiled by their 4-letter code. It pushes rather than overlays, for E-03's reason: you read the list and the card against each other.
Middle — the open card
The full M-01 field set grouped Identity / Ordering / Terms / Contact / Billing / Shipping / Notes, with the Log on the floor of the same scroll — the same place History sits on the Customer card (E-07 d16). Every field is a live input except Discount, which is gated.
Right — the ledger (sticky)
Four things in priority order: what is in flight with them, what is owed as a single figure, what has moved both ways, and what came in lately. No row-by-row ledger — that is Accounts Payable's screen and it is one click away. The foot is the errand you are actually on.

Why in-flight sits above the money

On the Customer card, Waiting outranks the balance because a hold does not expire and nothing chases it (E-07 d19, E-05 d7). The supplier-side equivalent of a stale hold is a draft Invoice left open, an overdue PO line, and a Pending claim nobody has followed up. All three go quiet the same way, and all three are things an Employee can act on — which is also why this band is the part of the track that survives the Employee/Manager toggle.

PO-line status is M-02's own: Pending and Ordered derived from whether a PO number exists, Shipped / Backordered / Cancelled set by a person (M-02 d12, d22). Nothing verifies an expected date — a date that has passed is a prompt to chase, not a state.

The foot is a ladder, like d20's

E-07 d20 made the Customer card's primary action a ladder — open the hold, else attach to the Sale in flight, else start a new one. The supplier equivalent, in the same shape:

  1. Open draft Invoice — there is one sitting half-received. It is the thing that goes stale.
  2. Process order — lines are pending against them and the stream is at its minimum (M-01 fields, M-02 d4).
  3. Start an intake — nothing in flight, so the errand is a new Invoice.

For a Manager, Record a payment rides underneath as a secondary when there is a balance — the same slot the Customer card gives "Attach to the Sale in flight instead."

What "Received $" has to decide first

You asked for a total Received figure. The record already carries two different numbers that could answer to that name, and they are not close:

  • Cost of goods — subtotal + freight + misc, inbound tax excluded (A-29, E-02 d34), because GST and QST are Input Tax Credits, a receivable rather than a cost.
  • Invoice total — what is actually owed the Supplier, tax included (E-02:98).

The mock shows cost of goods, and says so on the figure, because the number sitting next to a Sold figure is a cost-of-goods number or the margin beside it is wrong. The Invoice total is the one that reconciles to the A/P balance above it — so the two figures in this track are deliberately not the same basis, and the caveat line says that out loud.

What "Sold $" cannot see

Sold is summed over copies whose Invoice traces back to this Supplier. Two gaps, both structural rather than fixable by the query:

  • Copies with no Invoice behind them — an oversold copy minted straight from a Sale (E-05 d21), or stock that predates the system. They attribute to no Supplier and drop out silently, so the figure names how many it dropped.
  • Margin is overstated, by roughly 2.6% on the reference invoice: freight, tax and misc stay at Invoice level and are never allocated to items (E-02:100, M-03:60). M-03 requires any margin reporting to state that caveat, so this track states it.

There is also a scope line here worth naming rather than stepping over: trend and margin reporting beyond the daily close is listed out of scope (PRD §7:241) — "a reporting surface of its own." Two totals on a supplier card is not that surface, but it is the first step onto it.

The slab row, on two lines

It was one line of three cells: code, name, figure — with the grey meta line sharing the middle column and ending its ellipsis eight pixels from the money. At the widths a real account number reaches, the two read as one run of text.

It is four cells on two rows now. Name and figure on the first, the meta line and the figure's caption on the second — so the grey text has the full width under the name, and "we owe" still sits under the number it belongs to. Nothing was dropped; the in-flight count joined the meta line, since there is now room for it.

Bands belong to the sort now, not to the list. A–Z is the default, and sorting A–Z means you are looking someone up by name — a band across it splits the alphabet in two, so the name you want is in one of two places instead of one. That is a cost with no matching benefit. Bands appear only under Outstanding A/P, where the split is the ordering, and the Owing chip covers wanting just that band. Customers' rule still holds on top: a band that does not actually split the list does not render.

The in-flight cap

You were right that it was unbounded. F.A.B. now carries six — two drafts, two POs, two claims — which is an ordinary week for a distributor, and at full height it pushed the A/P figure clean off the track. The band is capped at three rows and scrolls inside itself, with a shadow on the bottom edge so there is a visible reason to scroll.

Grouped by kind, oldest first inside each — drafts, then orders, then claims. Not too much: the three kinds open on three different screens, so grouping them is also grouping by where the row takes you, and a flat oldest-first list would have you jumping between Receiving, What's on Order and Claims in whatever order the dates happened to fall. The order of the groups is a priority order too — a draft is ours to finish, a PO is theirs to ship, a claim is theirs to answer.

The grouping is what makes the cap safe. Flat, a cap of three could show three drafts and leave a nine-day-old claim below the fold with nothing to announce it. So the counts moved into the band's label, above the scroll, per kind: 2 drafts · 2 on order · 2 claims. You can see a claim exists without scrolling to it. A cap that hides how much it is hiding is worse than no cap.

Two addresses, one checkbox

Better than the remit-to flag it replaces, because it puts the fact in fields rather than in a note somebody has to read. F.A.B. is the case: accounts office is a Toronto box number, the warehouse is on Rue Guy in Montréal. Under the old flag that lived in free text, where nothing could ever consume it.

Ticked, the shipping fields mirror billing and are locked — greyed and italic, so they read as derived rather than as blank-and-broken. A mirrored field that is still typable is a field that silently stops mirroring the moment someone corrects the billing address, and then the two disagree with nothing to say which is right. Editing billing while it is ticked moves shipping with it.

Unticking keeps the copied values rather than blanking them, because "nearly the same address, different unit" is the usual reason to untick, and it puts the cursor in line 1. Three of the four seeded suppliers are ticked — which is the honest ratio, and the reason the checkbox defaults on.

Which one is which matters for a Supplier in a way it does not for a Customer: billing is where we send money, shipping is where their stock comes from. The group heads say so out loud, so neither gets read as one of the store's own addresses.

Every row is a way in

The in-flight rows and the recent-intake rows are buttons, not text — each opens the thing it names on the screen that owns it. Hover one and it says where it goes.

A draft, or a past Invoice → Receiving
Receiving history is already a first-class browsable screen and past Invoices open as they were finalized (E-02 d36, A-27), and the Invoice is already in the URL as /receiving/:invoiceId — so this is a deep link to something that exists, not a new surface.
A PO → What's on Order
Where a line gets re-flagged or its status set (M-02 d12, d22).
A claim → Claims
Where it is chased or marked Credited (E-04).
The A/P figure → Accounts Payable
The row-by-row ledger came out of this track entirely. M-05 already shows outstanding Invoices, claims and manual entries in one combined list per supplier (M-05 d3) — restating it here meant two places to read the same rows, and only one of them could act on them.

What I did not invent

  • Whether deleting a Supplier is manager-only. M-01 leaves it open on purpose — A-28a gates "deletions" as an unnamed category, the lexicon's manager-only entry omits deletions entirely, and E-07 d12 explicitly ungates deleting a Customer. Delete is drawn ungated for both viewers with no opinion attached.
  • What the addresses are for. M-01's field table has no address at all — both blocks are new, and the groups say so on the screen. They are drawn in the shape CustomerAddress already uses so there is one address shape in the system rather than two. Nothing consumes either yet: billing is captured for M-05, shipping for E-04's claim correspondence.
  • Currency conversion. Indie Direct is USD in seed. M-05 requires the store-currency equivalent at the configured rate (M-05:80, M-06). The mock shows both figures and does not invent a rate — it shows the placeholder the settings screen would fill.
  • What Merge does to the totals. Merge repoints every dependent pointer rather than rewriting history (M-01 d9) — so a merged Supplier's Received and Sold figures absorb the other's. Whether that is what a Manager expects is a real question and nobody has answered it.
  • Lifetime figures. The Customer card carries this-year / last-year / lifetime. This one carries 12 months only, because no decision says what a Supplier "year" is and the fiscal-year question belongs to a reporting surface, not here.
