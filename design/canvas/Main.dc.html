The one thing you added
“Helps find things they have had in the past very fast vs things that are in store right now.”
That is a requirement, not a style note, and the two examples answer it differently. It also lines up with E-03: catalogue rows and titles we have held before are already in scope for search — nobody had said how to tell them apart at a glance.
Why there are two, and not one
B is high-contrast and industrial. C is warm and editorial. E is a neutral tool. You picked structure from all three, which is fine — but only one of them can supply the voice, or the app ends up looking like three apps. Each example below settles that question a different way, and pairs it with a different answer to the history-versus-stock question.
1 · Paper
C supplies the voice
Warm ground, a serif for record titles, oxblood for action. B's big till structure and E's rails are rendered in that palette rather than their own.
Find answers by grouping. Results are split into labelled bands — on the floor, on order, stocked before, catalogue only. The answer is the heading you land in.
2 · Signal
B supplies the voice
Achromatic chrome, one orange reserved for the primary action. Colour appears only on state and on cover art, so the sleeve is the brightest thing on screen.
Find answers by history. One ranked list with filter chips and a recency stamp per row, and a titlecard that shows what you paid and charged the last three times you stocked it.
The layouts are identical between the two. Same top menu, same till skeleton, same worklist rail, same three-zone Find. Only the voice and the Find behaviour change, so the comparison is clean.
