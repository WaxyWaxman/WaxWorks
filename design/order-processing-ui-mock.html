What changed, and why layout

Today Order Processing is a page with two stacked tables and four modals — Process, View, Void, and the merge/delete confirmations that open on top of View. The frame below keeps every one of those jobs and loses the stacking.

  • Track 1 retracts to a 52px strip carrying the search, a ready count, and one tile per stream and PO — the same strip Find, Receive, Customers, Suppliers and On Order already collapse to.
  • Track 2 is the work surface, with three faces: every pending stream (step 4's table, as rows), one stream's lines editable in place (step 5's View, no longer a modal), or a placed PO read-only.
  • Track 3 is pinned and carries the verb. Its floor holds the PO number field and Process for a pending stream, Void for a placed PO — in the same spot the primary action sits on every other screen.
  • Highlighting a line swaps track 3 to the titlecard, which is what step 5 asks for and what the modal-in-a-modal was doing badly.
  • Processing takes tracks 2 and 3 rather than opening over them — the same move the bulk order sheet makes on What's on Order. The document you are about to send is worth the full width; a confirm dialog is not.

Two modals survive, and should. Voiding a PO and merging two separators are both one-way and both need a plan read before they are agreed to (d17, d24). They stay dialogs.

Recorded, and shown here M-02

  • One row per supplier + separator, with pending total, age of oldest, order via, customer-attached count, sell total, estimated cost, and readiness — step 4, d4.
  • Placed POs listed most-recent-first — step 4.
  • View shows progress to the minimum against whichever one the supplier names, and is where qty, sell price and separator are edited and a line deleted — step 5, d9.
  • Highlighting a line opens its titlecard — step 5.
  • Email sends; every other method prints and is marked placed — step 6, d6.
  • PO number blank auto-assigns ascending, skipping anything in use; manual is free text — step 7, d5, d16.
  • Placement queues the catalog prefetch — step 8, d14.
  • Separator collision prompts to merge, one-way — d17, both per line and per stream.
  • Void returns unreceived lines, splits part-received ones, tells the supplier nothing — d10, d11, d24.
  • A placed line is never deleted — d21; outstanding is counted across Invoices — d15.

What this mock invents needs a decision row

None of these are in M-02. Each would need a row appended before it is built.

  1. The slab searches titles as well as suppliers. Today the filter is supplier name or short code only. Matching an artist or title and filtering to the streams containing it answers “is that on the next order?” — but it is a new behaviour.
  2. Chips: Ready / Waiting / Aging / Email. Nothing records a filter set for this screen. Ready and Waiting come straight off step 4's columns; Aging needs a threshold nobody has set.
  3. The readiness meter. Step 4 records Ready as a yes/no. The bar shows the distance, which is what a manager deciding whether to wait actually needs. Presentation of a recorded fact — but new chrome.
  4. Sorting streams by readiness, age, supplier or cost. Step 4 gives no order for the pending table at all. Readiness-first is a proposal.
  5. “Placed elsewhere” POs marked their ref here. d25 creates them and On Order marks them; this screen's placed table does not yet, and the same reasoning applies.
  6. The pile summary in track 3's all-streams face. Totals across every pending stream are not a recorded figure. Cheap to show, and nothing reads it.

Deliberately not invented: no scan box. On Order has one because step 10 asks for it. Phase 2 asks for nothing of the kind, and a barcode is not how you pick a stream.

Questions for you before code

  1. Pending and placed in one slab list, or a toggle? Shown as one list with two bands, so voiding a PO and processing a stream are the same screen. A toggle would halve the scroll and hide half the screen.
  2. Should track 2's all-streams face list streams, or every pending line? Streams, here — step 4 says one row per supplier + separator, and the stream is the unit of action. On Order lists lines because Phase 3's unit is the line.
  3. Does Process belong in track 3's floor or track 2's head? Floor, here, to match every other screen. It does mean the send button and the line you are reading are at opposite corners.
  4. The expand arrow is shared chrome. The strip now opens on a ⟩ mirroring the ⟨ that closed it. The other five slabs — Find, Receive, Customers, Suppliers, On Order — still open on a magnifier. Either this screen diverges, or the arrow propagates to all six. Recommend all six.

Answered — and one of them found a gap needs decision rows

A short stream just waits. Confirmed: a small shop can take months to reach a distributor's minimum, and that is ordinary, not a fault. So nothing ages a pending stream out, nothing nags, nothing auto-sends. Worth a row anyway, because the consequence is not obvious from the screen:

Proposed d31. A pending stream below its supplier's minimum waits indefinitely. Nothing expires it, escalates it, or sends it short. Accepted consequence: a customer-attached line can sit in a stream that may never reach the minimum, and nothing tells the customer — the age column is the only signal, and nobody is obliged to read it. Status: recommended, not yet ratified.

The duplicate mark is the wrong tool for the case you described — and the case you described is the better question.

d29's mark is about accidental double entry while scanning a stack into the bulk sheet. What you described is deliberate: one speculative copy and one for a named customer, raised separately, as two lines of qty 1 rather than one line of qty 2. That is what the spec already does — M-02:35 says a raised line “joins that supplier's pending pile” and nothing merges it — and it is right, because the two copies are different commitments. One is spoken for; the other is a bet.

The real gap is the next step. Nothing anywhere records detaching a customer from an order line. Attaching is recorded (E-04:36, Reserve). Detaching a scanned copy from a PO line at the receiving desk is recorded (M-02 d20, E-02:30). Detaching a person is not recorded anywhere in the set. So when the customer rings and cancels:

  • Delete the line (d9, M-02:89) — the only recorded route. Kills the order outright. Wrong if you would have stocked one anyway.
  • Do nothing — the line keeps their name, and on receipt d13 (M-02:81) automatically creates a Held Sale for them. A hold for someone who cancelled. And holds do not expire (E-05 d7, E-05:146), so it sits on the shelf until a person notices.
  • There is no third option.
Proposed d32. A Customer may be detached from a pending order line, leaving the line in place, unattached, at the same quantity. Detaching is not deleting: the copy may still be wanted. The line's log records who detached and when (d23). Why it is needed: without it the only recorded response to a cancelled request is deleting the line (d9), and the only alternative is leaving an attachment that d13 will turn into a non-expiring Held Sale for someone who cancelled. Open inside it: whether detaching on a placed line is also allowed — d21 says a placed line is never deleted, which is an argument that detach is exactly the right verb there. Status: recommended, not yet ratified.

Both rows belong to M-02 and neither is a layout question — the mock can be built without them. But d32 is a live hole in the flow, not a refinement, so it is worth closing before the code teaches people the delete-it habit.
