What each track is for

Left — the customer slab (retractable)
Search over name, email and phone (d14), chips, a sort control, the list. Shut, a 52px strip carrying New, Search with a result badge, and the cards you have opened. It pushes rather than overlays, the way Find's and Receive's do.
Middle — the open card
Every field a live input, grouped Identity / Contact / Address / Note, with History on the floor of the same scroll (d16). No Edit function, no confirm step (d13), so the head carries a saved chip instead.
Right — the account (sticky)
Now four things in priority order: what is waiting for them, what is owed either way, who they are to the store, and what is in flight. The foot is the errand you are actually on.

The right track, rebuilt

You were right that it was all balance, and most customers never carry one. Theo is the case that exposed it — a perfectly ordinary regular whose whole track said $0.00.

Waiting moved above the balance. E-05 says holds do not expire and the timeline records age because "a hold nobody has chased is otherwise invisible" (d7). The customer card is where it stops being invisible, so it gets the top of the track — and it carries the age and the contact preference, which is what you need in order to act on it.

The balance shrinks when it is zero. It still owns the second slot, because it is the figure that can be wrong in a way that costs money (d4) — but a settled account renders at 24px instead of spending the top of the screen on the least informative number on it.

Standing, On order and Recent sales fill the rest. On order carries M-02's real statuses: Pending and Ordered derived from whether a PO number exists, Shipped, Backordered and Cancelled set by a person (d12, d22), with the supplier's expected date where there is one.

Held sale beats attach — and a question underneath it

The foot is now a ladder, and it tells you what it skipped:

  • Something held → Open hold H4, with the in-flight Sale offered underneath as a secondary.
  • Sale in flight, nothing held → Attach to Sale #1043.
  • Neither → New Sale with Ramona.

Your second reading — fold the in-flight Sale into the hold — I did not draw, because it is a new operation and it collides with two recorded rows. E-05 d23 locks an Open Sale to the Employee who opened it, and E-05 d3 gives a Held Sale an H-reference that becomes a Sale number on tender. A merge has to say which lock survives, which reference survives, and what becomes of the absorbed Sale's log. That is its own decision row, not a layout choice — worth raising properly if you want it, and cheap to defer either way, since the recorded move (open the hold, ring the extra copies onto it) already gets everything into one transaction.

Sorting by surname — pick one

You cannot sort by last name today: the record has a single name field (Fields table; types.ts), so "last name" means guessing the final token — which files Left Bank Cafe (wholesale) under W.

a — leave it, sort the whole string
What I drew. At a counter you type into the search box; you almost never browse alphabetically, so the sort is a convenience rather than the path. Free.
b — split into given / family name
Correct, and awkward: business accounts have no surname, so you get an empty field on every Business card and a rule about which one sorts. Amends the Fields table.
c — one name field plus an optional "file under" (my pick)
How record shops already alphabetise — Beatles, The. One optional field, blank by default, falling back to the name when empty, and business accounts simply never set it. Adds a field without splitting one.

The sort control is drawn with A–Z · Recently seen · Spend this year, which works under any of the three.

The waiting band, and the filter you asked for

Waiting is a chip and a band. Customers with something held sort into a Waiting to collect band above everyone else — which is Find's structure exactly, and it answers "what orders the list" at the same time. The chip filters to that band alone.

The sort applies inside the bands, not across them — again as Find's does. Switch to Spend, this year and Ramona stays on top because she is the one with something to collect, while the band below reorders. If you would rather the sort beat the band outright, that is a one-line change, but then the waiting band stops being a band and becomes a mere label.

The chip row now carries two axes — a live state and a stored field — so there is a divider between them rather than five chips reading as one list of equivalent things.

No owes-us filter, as agreed. Worth noting that Waiting does the job you would have wanted it for anyway: the person you need to chase is almost always the one with something sitting on the hold shelf, not the one carrying a balance.

One thing I left alone: the shut strip's tiles still carry a balance dot, not a waiting mark. Two-datum rule. If waiting is the more useful signal there, say so and I will swap which one the dot means.

Where the spend figures come from

Not invented, but not specified either — worth knowing exactly where they stand before we build them.

  • Recorded as a goal. PRD G-4 — admins "review individual customer behavior to provide incentives and credit" — and the Manager's jobs include determining "who their top customers are by quantity or life-time value". So the store has asked for this.
  • Not recorded anywhere as a field. No flow implements it. E-07's Fields table has no spend figure, and d16 scopes History to sold items only.
  • The boundary is NG-2: there is no loyalty program. These are reporting figures. The moment they become tiers, thresholds or automatic discounts, they have crossed a stated non-goal.
  • One tension to settle. The PRD frames customer value as a Manager/Admin job, but E-07 d12 says nothing about a Customer is gated. I have shown the figures to Employees, which is consistent with d12 — but it does put a manager-framed number on a counter screen a customer can read over your shoulder. Same argument as Find's cost toggle.

New stops being a modal

You asked whether there is a more natural choice. I do not think so — d13 says the card is the edit surface, so New and open being the same surface is only that rule applied one step further.

But there is a better entry point than a ＋ button, and it is wired in the mock: search for someone who is not there, and the empty state offers New customer named "…" with the name already filled. That is the real counter sequence — you look first, and only then discover they need creating. The ＋ stays for when you already know.

What I did not invent

  • No age at which a hold looks overdue. E-05 records that age is tracked, not a threshold. The band states "held 12 days" and leaves the judgement to whoever reads it. If you want it to go ochre at n days, n needs a decision row.
  • The movement list is still an inference. d5 says the balance is derived from movements; it never says the movements are visible. It hides entirely when there are none.
  • No merge, no credit limit. Both are E-07 open questions, shown rather than solved — nothing stops Left Bank's −$120.50.
  • Issue customer invoice is shape only (d10); terms are open.

Rows I would append

d17. Customers is a fixed three-track frame — slab, card, account — matching E-05 d29, E-03 and E-02 d38. The frame never scrolls; each track does. Below 1180px the account track drops to a bottom strip.

d18 — refines d13. New opens a blank card in the middle track rather than a modal, so New and an open card are the same surface. A search matching nothing offers New with the typed name pre-filled.

d19. The account track carries, in order: what is waiting for them (Held Sales and customer-attached order lines, with age and contact preference), the signed balance, standing (spend this year, last year, lifetime, sale count), the two till-affecting fields, on-order lines with M-02 status, and recent sales. Waiting sits above the balance because a hold does not expire and nothing else chases it (E-05 d7). Spend figures are reporting only — NG-2 stands.

d20. The card's primary action is a ladder: open a Held Sale, else attach to a Sale in flight, else start a new Sale. Merging an in-flight Sale into a hold is explicitly not part of this and needs its own row (E-05 d23, d3).
