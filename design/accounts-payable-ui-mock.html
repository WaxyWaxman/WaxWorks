What M-05 would have to say

The layout needs no decisions. Three tracks, a retractable slab and a sticky right column are how this app already works — E-05 d29 by way of E-02 d38, E-07 d17, M-01 d17, and most recently M-02 d31 for Order Processing. Folding the three modals into track 3 changes no behaviour: Record payment, Clear selected and Create new do exactly what d4, d5, d12–d16 already say, in a place that does not cover the ledger they are about.

One row genuinely needs appending, and it is small. M-05 says of the Supplier's billing address, in as many words, that “Nothing here consumes it yet.” This layout consumes it — the remit-to address sits in track 3 while you are writing down a cheque number. That is a change to a recorded statement, so it is a decision row, not a detail.

What this is worth. Not tidiness. Today the Manager ticks rows and then guesses which of two buttons will light up, with the reason hidden in a title attribute. Here the right track reads the selection back — what it sums to, which action it is legal for, and why the other one is not. The failure this prevents is the one M-05 d15 is most exposed to: Clear is manual reconciliation with no undo described anywhere in the flow, and a selection that does not sum to zero should be refused out loud rather than silently.

Proposed rowWording
M-05 d17 not ratified The Supplier's billing address is displayed on the accounts payable screen as the remit-to address, alongside the payment being recorded. Extends the M-01 d13 line inherited into this flow, which captures a billing address and notes that nothing here consumes it. Recording a payment is the moment the store asks “where does the cheque go”, and the answer is already on file one screen away. It is display only: nothing is printed, addressed, or sent, and M-01 remains the only place the address is edited. Accepted consequence: a stale billing address now looks authoritative at the moment it is most likely to be acted on, and nothing on this screen can tell that it is stale — the Supplier card is still the only place it is maintained.

What each track is for

Left — the payables slab (retractable)
The store-wide figure at the top, then the lookup, then the chips, then the sort, then who is owed. Two controls that do different jobs and look different: chips filter the list (their counts are suppliers), a supplier row scopes the middle to that supplier's ledger. The lookup is not a filter of the list — it reaches any Supplier including one with no payables at all, which is step 1's explicit requirement and the only way to start a Create new against a supplier you have never owed. Shut, a 52px strip carries Create new, a credits badge, and the suppliers tiled by their short code.
Middle — the combined ledger
Decision 3's one list — Invoices, Claims and manual entries together, because what is owed is the net of both. A real table rather than On Order's row grid: six money columns that have to line up is what this screen is for. Each row is a selection, not a navigation.
Right — the selection (sticky)
Three states, in strict priority: the new entry you are composing, else the selection you have ticked, else the supplier's standing. All three modals collapse into it.

Why the right track is the selection

On Order's track 3 is the line; Customers' is the customer. The equivalent here is not one row — it is however many rows you have ticked, because every action in M-05 acts on a set. A payment covers a mix (d16); clearing needs at least two (d15); applying a credit spreads across all of them (d11).

So the track reads the set back and names the one action it is legal for. Ticked invoices → per-target amounts, method, reference, date. Ticked entries summing to zero → clear, with d15's “this changes nothing about the balance” said where you can read it. Anything else → a red block that says the sum and what is wrong with it. The two disabled buttons and their tooltips go away.

One band, and the bands not drawn

On Order bands its list on d19's overdue grouping. The equivalent line through a payables ledger is d14: a Claim placeholder does not move the balance until it is cleared against a matching Credit. Today that is a word in a Status column; here it is a band, and the figures above and below it mean different things.

There are no aging bands. M-05 lists aging — 30/60/90 — as an open question, and specifically as one whose home is undecided: here, or in a general reporting surface. A mock that drew the buckets would answer that question by drawing it. Each row carries its age in days, and the slab offers oldest unpaid first as a sort; that is data and ordering, not a report, and neither commits the flow.

A thing the bands turned up

In the screen as it stands the ledger's Balance column does not add up to the owed badge above it, and it cannot, because the column means two different things depending on the row.

A Credited-but-unapplied Supplier Claim renders as -amount at screens/AccountsPayable.tsx:140, and a Claim placeholder renders its signed face value at screens/AccountsPayable.tsx:149 — but the net owed is computed from invoiceBalance plus payableEntryContribution, and neither of those rows contributes to it (lib/totals.ts:150, :194). The figures are each defensible on their own; the column is not, because it mixes “what this contributes” with “what this is worth for clearing”.

The code is right and the layout was hiding it. M-05 step 6 has the Manager mark a claim Credited and then apply it, as two acts; d14 keeps a placeholder out of the balance entirely. Three bands, each with its own subtotal, make the table sum to the headline again — which is why this mock draws them, and why the fix is layout rather than arithmetic. Not a decision: a reading of d6, d11 and d14 that the current screen does not make visible.

Why gift cards moved into the slab

Today the registry is a full-width table under the supplier grid, which reads as an afterthought bolted to a screen about suppliers. But d10 puts it here for a reason that is structural: it is money owed, and so is everything else in the slab. Making it the last destination in that list — under a rule, tiled in black so it never reads as a Supplier — says what d10 means without adding a word to the flow.

Selecting it scopes the middle track to the registry and the right track to the total liability. Nothing about it is editable here: loading and redeeming happen at the till (E-05), and this is the register, not the mechanism.

Two totals, not one

The slab's store-wide figure is one line per currency, not a sum. M-05 requires a Supplier's currency to carry through, with the store-currency equivalent shown at the configured rate — and that rate lives in M-06, which is not built. Adding CAD 282.45 to USD 23.75 would be inventing an exchange rate in the one place on the screen most likely to be read as authoritative.

When M-06 lands, this becomes a single figure with the per-currency lines beneath it. Until then the honest shape is the one that cannot be wrong. The same restraint applies inside a foreign-currency supplier's ledger, where the caveat sits in track 3 rather than a fabricated conversion sitting in the table.

The credit lands before you press it

The top slot of track 3 — where On Order puts who is waiting — here holds a credit nobody has applied. Same reasoning: of everything true about this supplier, that is the one thing that is money on the table and the only one a person has to go and do.

d11 already specifies exactly where it will land: distributed across outstanding Invoices, oldest received first, absorbing any remainder on the last one. Since the algorithm is settled, the band can show the landing — which invoices, how much on each — before the button is pressed. Today that distribution is described in a title tooltip and only observable afterwards.

What is mock data

The prototype's seed carries two open Invoices, one Credited claim and one Adjustment — enough to prove the store works, not enough to show a layout. This mock adds, for display only: a second F.A.B. invoice carrying a partial payment (so d4 has something to show), a Claim placeholder and the Credit that matches it (so d14's band and d15's clearing have rows), and one consignment Supplier, Vinyl Revival, so M-01's consignment flag and d13's type default are visible.

None of that is a proposal about seed data. When this is built, the screen should render whatever the store holds, including the empty cases — which is why the settled supplier, the zero-balance card, and the reachable-but-inactive Walk-in / trade-in are all still in here.

Open, and deliberately left open

  • A cross-supplier view. On Order's slab has a permanent Everything on order first row. This one has no equivalent, on purpose. A combined ledger implies a payment run across suppliers, and both d7 (a claim applies only within one supplier) and d16's PaymentBatch are supplier-scoped as written. Building the view first and discovering the model second is the wrong order. Recorded as an open question, not designed around.
  • Aging. Still open, still unanswered here — see above.
  • Undoing a clear. d15 says nothing is deleted and both entries stay in the ledger as history, but does not say whether a clearing can be reversed. The mock therefore does not offer it, and the confirmation in track 3 does not promise it can be undone.
  • Who the reference belongs to. d5 makes the payment reference free text because the bank statement is the reconciliation surface. Whether the screen should warn on a duplicate reference within one supplier is not decided, and is left alone.
  • Currency movement. An invoice raised at one rate and paid at another is M-05's own open question. The payment composer records the amount in the supplier's currency and says nothing more.
