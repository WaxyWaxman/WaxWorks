# E-01 — Authenticate to the platform

**Actor:** Employee (steps 6 and 7 are a Manager's or an Owner's)
**Status:** Specified
**Related:** [M-04 Manage users](M-04-manage-users.md) · [M-06 Configure the store](M-06-settings.md) · [O-01 Administer the organization](O-01-administer-the-organization.md) · [S-01 Onboard and recover organizations](S-01-onboard-and-recover-organizations.md)

**Job:** As an employee, I need to identify myself to the system so my actions are attributed to me and I can access the till.

**Scope note:** This flow is how a person becomes known to a terminal. Who may be a User, which Stores they are assigned to, and who sets a PIN or sends an invite is [M-04](M-04-manage-users.md)'s; a Store's own account is [M-06](M-06-settings.md)'s and the Organization around it is the Owner's ([O-01](O-01-administer-the-organization.md)).

---

## Flow

0. A terminal opens a **store session** by signing in with the Store's **store account** — the Store's own email and password (decision 24). The store session is shared and always-on: it does not lapse, it is what scopes everything the terminal can reach to that Store and its Organization, and it is not a staff sign-in.
1. A **terminal** presents the till on its store session with no staff session open.
2. Employee enters their **initials** to open a session on that terminal. Identification **resolves as you type** (decision 19), among the people **assigned to** this Store (decision 25): no Enter, and Escape cancels whatever asked.
3. The session stays active on that terminal for a configured period of inactivity — **defaulting to 5 minutes** ([M-06](M-06-settings.md) d45, replacing decision 4's flat 15) — then lapses. An **Open Sale suppresses the lapse** on that terminal (decision 10).
4. While a session is active, every action taken on that terminal is attributed to that Employee.
5. When no session is active, any action requiring attribution prompts for initials inline and proceeds without opening a full session. On a card that saves field by field, that prompt is **once per card** rather than once per field (decision 20).
5a. **Some actions prompt every time, session or not** (decisions 12 and 15): opening a new Sale, recording a pay-out, adjusting on hand, and voiding. Starting a Return is not a fourth case — a Return *is* a Sale with negative lines ([E-06](E-06-process-a-return.md) steps 1-2), so it prompts as a new Sale does (decision 15). Receiving, order processing and the rest of the back office are covered by the session, because the same person works those for an hour at a stretch.
6. A Manager or Owner authorizing a **manager-only** action, or crossing into a manager-locked area, enters their four-digit **PIN** at that point — **always, on a store session** (decision 26; decision 23 as amended); this does not replace the Employee's session. Actions that formerly needed a *manager override* now proceed and raise a review flag instead ([M-04](M-04-manage-users.md) d8).
7. A Manager or Owner may instead open a **personal session** — signed in as themselves with email, password and a second factor (decisions 27, 28) — pick one of their Stores, and work with nothing asking for initials or a PIN: every action is attributed to them, and nothing lapses.

---

## What a terminal is

A **terminal** is a browser session on a device. Two browsers on the same physical machine are two terminals, each with an independent staff session. Sessions are not shared between devices, and signing in at the counter does not sign anyone in at the receiving desk.

A terminal holds at most one **store session**, and keeps a name of its own — *Till 1* — for the drawer it opens and the receipts it prints. A **personal session** is a person's, not a terminal's: it is opened on whatever device the Manager or Owner is at, and a device holding one is not a terminal in this sense.

---

## Credentials

**Three ways in, one per principal.** A **store account** — the Store's own email and password — opens a store session on a terminal, the shared counter (decision 24). On it, Employees are attribution by initials, and Managers and Owners prove themselves with a four-digit **PIN** at the manager-only line (decisions 25, 26). A **personal session** is a Manager or Owner signed in as themselves, with email, password and a second factor (decisions 27, 28). A System Administrator signs in with a passkey and reaches identity data only (`S-` series). The optional barrier password of decision 21 is retired (decision 29) — nothing in this model has a place for it.

The shape decision 18 was built to protect held: actions are still attributed to a **User**, a store session still belongs to a terminal, and manager authorization is still distinct from the staff session. What changed is how a session is opened and what proves a Manager — and decision 30 walks decision 18's checklist item by item.

**What the database trusts is the session it issued — to the store account or to the person — and never the initials** (decision 24; [architecture](../architecture.md) A-87, which supersedes A-3). A store session carries the Organization, the Store and the terminal; a personal session carries the Organization, the person, and the Store they picked. Initials remain attribution on top of a store session and are still not a credential, which is what keeps decision 16's reuse tolerable (decision 30). The PIN *is* a credential, and it is checked where the write happens, not in the browser — every manager-only function on a store session takes the PIN and resolves it itself ([architecture](../architecture.md) A-89).

## Requirements

- Every Sale, Return, void, hold cancellation, pay-out, inventory adjustment, and Invoice finalization is attributed to a User.
- A lapsed session must never silently reattribute actions to the previous Employee.
- **Draft Invoices must survive a session lapse mid-scan** (see Inherited). Receiving is a long operation and a timeout must not discard work.
- A User belongs to an Organization and is **assigned to** one or more of its Stores (decision 25, [M-04](M-04-manage-users.md) d27). On a store session, initials and PINs resolve among the people assigned to that Store and nobody else.
- Historical attribution survives a User being deactivated — see [M-04](M-04-manage-users.md).
- Initials are unique among a Store's active, assigned Users (decisions 14, 25), and released when a User is deactivated (decision 16). PINs are unique among the Managers and Owners assigned to a Store (decision 26).
- A deactivation stops new work under those initials at once at the write path, and the shell drops the session at its next actor re-resolution; only an already-Open Sale outlives it ([M-04](M-04-manage-users.md) d15, d18). A personal session the deactivated User holds ends with it (decision 30).
- A store session always asks for a PIN at the manager-only line; a personal session never asks for a PIN or for initials (decisions 26, 27).
- No credential is ever typed by an administrator: a PIN is set by an Owner or Manager, a password by its holder through an emailed link, a store account's by an Owner ([M-04](M-04-manage-users.md) d28, d29; [M-06](M-06-settings.md) d70).

## Inherited from other flows

**From [E-02](E-02-receive-inventory.md):**

**From [E-05](E-05-sell-a-record.md) and [E-04](E-04-manage-inventory.md):**

- A **manager-only authorization** must be performable at a terminal that currently has an Employee session open, without ending that session. (This was written for the manager override, which [M-04](M-04-manage-users.md) d8 has since replaced with a review queue; the requirement survives for the manager-only list.)

**From [M-04](M-04-manage-users.md):**

- **Deactivating a User stops new work under their initials at once, and the screen follows within one actor re-resolution** ([M-04](M-04-manage-users.md) d15 as corrected by d18, [architecture](../architecture.md) A-55). There is no server-side session to end — under [architecture](../architecture.md) A-3 and A-50 a session is a timer in a browser — so the enforcement is `actor_resolve` refusing the User at the write path, and the shell catching up when it next re-resolves. It matters because decision 13 leaves the lapse unbounded: waiting for a lapse could leave a deactivated User attributing work for an hour.
- **An Open Sale survives the deactivation** and stays finishable, rather than being force-unlocked and raising a flag ([E-05](E-05-sell-a-record.md) d23, [architecture](../architecture.md) A-28). The session is gone; the Sale in front of the customer is not.
- **A deactivated User's initials are released** ([M-04](M-04-manage-users.md) d16), which is why decision 14's uniqueness holds among active Users only.
- ~~**A Store always has at least one active Manager** ([M-04](M-04-manage-users.md) d14), so there is always somebody who can authorize a manager-only action at a terminal.~~ — **superseded by [M-04](M-04-manage-users.md) d26:** the floor is the Organization's — at least one active **Owner** — and a Store may have no Manager assigned. On such a Store's store session nobody's PIN opens the manager-only line; an Owner does that work on a personal session, or assigns themselves and uses their own PIN.
- **PINs are set by an Owner or a Manager; passwords by their holder through an emailed link** ([M-04](M-04-manage-users.md) d28, d29). This flow asks for them; it never sets them.
- **What a Manager may administer is bounded to Employees at their own Stores** ([M-04](M-04-manage-users.md) d30). It matters here because the manager-only line is the same line for a Manager and an Owner; what differs is what is offered once across it.

**From [M-06](M-06-settings.md):**

- **The session lapse is a store setting defaulting to 5 minutes**, not the 15 of decision 4 ([M-06](M-06-settings.md) d45). The rule is unchanged — it measures *inactivity*, so an hour of continuous work never prompts and a six-minute absence does. Configurable because it is ergonomics rather than security: what it prevents is accidental misattribution. ~~**It changes character the day credentials arrive**, and must be revisited rather than inherited.~~ **Revisited by decision 30:** credentials arrived and the lapse did *not* change character, because the access is the store session and the lapse still only guards attribution on top of it. It governs staff sessions on a store session only — a personal session has no lapse (decision 27). **The setting takes no maximum** (decision 13), which declines A-50's standing recommendation.
- **A Store's store account is part of its details** ([M-06](M-06-settings.md) d70): its email and password are set and reset by an Owner and by nobody in the shop. Rotating the password signs every terminal of that Store out; that is the intended way to sign out a terminal nobody can reach.

## Open questions

- ~~**Credentials** — **deferred to a later version**, not open (decision 18).~~ — **Resolved** by decisions 24–30: store account, PIN, personal session with a second factor; decision 30 discharges decision 18's checklist.
- ~~**Multi-store membership** — **deferred to a later version**, not open.~~ — **Resolved** by decision 25 and [M-04](M-04-manage-users.md) d27: a User is assigned to one or more Stores of their Organization; initials are unique per Store among the assigned.
- **Does the store account need a second factor?** Decision 28 leaves it open. A shared terminal makes a phone code awkward and a remembered device plausible; a store account with no second factor makes the Store's email password the whole of the counter's access. Owned here.
- **May a Manager on a personal session pick a Store they are not assigned to?** Decision 27 says an Owner may pick any and a Manager one of theirs; whether a Manager may *read* a sister Store without being assigned is a question for the `O-` series, which owns assignment across the Organization.
- **A personal session on a shared machine.** Decision 27 names the hole and states the practice — the counter runs a store session — without enforcing it. Whether the product should refuse a personal session on a device that also holds a store session, or time one out, is open.
- ~~**Initials collisions.**~~ — **Resolved** by decision 14: initials are unique within a Store, enforced when [M-04](M-04-manage-users.md) creates the User. Whether **deactivated** Users hold their initials against reuse is part of that decision's detail and is recorded in M-04.
- ~~**Does the 15-minute lapse apply during an open Sale?**~~ — **Resolved** by decision 10: it does not. A Sale left part-rung on the counter keeps its terminal alive, and the Sale stays locked to the Employee who opened it ([E-05](E-05-sell-a-record.md) d23).
