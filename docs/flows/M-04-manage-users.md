# M-04 — Add/remove employees or managers

**Actor:** Manager (Owner for anything that touches a Manager or an Owner — decision 30)
**Status:** Specified
**Related:** [E-01 Authenticate](E-01-authenticate.md) · [M-06 Configure the store](M-06-settings.md) · [O-01 Administer the organization](O-01-administer-the-organization.md) · [S-01 Onboard and recover organizations](S-01-onboard-and-recover-organizations.md)

**Job:** As a manager, I need to control who has access to the system and at what level.

**Scope note:** This flow is the people of one Organization — their role, their Stores, their PIN, their standing. The Organization itself — its Stores, their store accounts, and the Owners — is [O-01](O-01-administer-the-organization.md); the Organization's creation and recovery is [S-01](S-01-onboard-and-recover-organizations.md).

---

## Roles

Three roles in an Organization, each a strict superset of the one before (decision 25). **Manager** has everything an **Employee** has; **Owner** has everything a Manager has, and administers the Organization besides — its Stores, its store accounts, its Managers and its Owners (`O-` series). A **System Administrator** is not a fourth role: WaxWorks staff, outside every Organization, reaching identity data and never store data (`S-` series).

A User belongs to the Organization and is **assigned to** one or more of its Stores (decision 27). On a store session, initials and PINs resolve among the people assigned to that Store.

The design intent is that Employees have high agency. The manager-gated list below is short on purpose, and everything not on it is available to any Employee. **User administration itself is on that list** ([architecture](../architecture.md) A-55) — it was omitted until A-55 found it, which, read against decision 2, made promoting yourself an Employee action. Within user administration, **what a Manager may touch is bounded to Employees at their own Stores** (decision 30); a Manager or an Owner is touched only by an Owner.

### Deliberately *not* gated

- **Discounting a line below cost at the till** ([E-05](E-05-sell-a-record.md) d12). A till discount is a one-off on a single Sale; a shelf price persists. Employees are trusted with the former.
- **Processing a Return** ([E-06](E-06-process-a-return.md) d3). No receipt, no time limit, no approval.
- **Creating a supplier** ([E-02](E-02-receive-inventory.md) d3) — but not setting its margin.

## The review queue

**The manager override is replaced by a review queue** (decision 8). Actions that used to stop and wait for a Manager's initials now **proceed**, and record a flag the Manager reviews afterward.

| Flagged action | Flow |
|---|---|
| A **shelf price** below cost | [E-02](E-02-receive-inventory.md) d35, [E-04](E-04-manage-inventory.md) |
| A derived-vs-stated **subtotal discrepancy** accepted | [E-02](E-02-receive-inventory.md) d18 |
| A Sale driving stock **negative** | [E-05](E-05-sell-a-record.md) |
| A **sale lock** broken on a stranded terminal | [E-05](E-05-sell-a-record.md) d23 |

A flag records the acting Employee, the subject, and the figures that made it worth a look — cost against price, the size of the delta, the variance. It is written in the same transaction as the action it describes, so the two can never disagree. Flags are **acknowledged, never deleted**.

This extends the design intent stated above: Employees have high agency, and a Manager sees what happened rather than standing in the way of it. It also answers what the audit-surface open question asked for.

### Manager-only authorization

The in-place mechanism survives for the manager-only table (decision 9). On a store session a Manager or Owner authorizes at the terminal where the Employee is working by entering their four-digit **PIN**, without ending or replacing the Employee's session ([E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d26, which supersedes the initials step of E-01 d6 and d11). Both names are retained — the record shows who did the thing and who authorized it. On a **personal session** — a Manager or Owner signed in as themselves — nothing asks: the session is the authorization ([E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d27), and the one name recorded is both names.

## Managing users

| Action | Behavior |
|---|---|
| **Add** | Creates a User with a role, initials and one or more Store assignments (decision 27). **Initials must be unique among the active Users assigned to each of those Stores** and Add refuses a duplicate, naming who holds them ([E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d25, decision 13) — resolved there and then. An Employee is added with no credential and no invite. A Manager or Owner is added with an email address and receives an **invite** to set their own password (decision 29), and is given a PIN (decision 28). A Manager may add Employees at their own Stores only; a Manager or Owner is added by an Owner (decision 30). |
| **Reactivate** | Restores the original User row, so one person keeps one history (decision 19). Prompts for new initials where theirs have been reused at any of their Stores. |
| **Assign** | Adds or removes a Store from a User's assignments (decision 27). Assigning refuses an initials clash and, for a Manager or Owner, a PIN clash at that Store, naming who holds it. Unassigning the last Store of an Employee or a Manager is refused; an Owner may hold none. |
| **PIN** | An Owner or a Manager sets, changes or clears a Manager's or Owner's four-digit PIN (decision 28). Unique among the Managers and Owners assigned to each of that person's Stores ([E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d26). Logged as changed, never as a value. Employees have none. |
| **Password** | Nobody sets another person's password. A Manager or Owner sets their own through the emailed invite, and resets it through an emailed link they or an administrator can trigger (decision 29). Logged as sent and as set, never as a value. |
| **Correct** | Fix a User's initials, display name or email at any time (decision 22). Uniqueness and normalisation still apply, the change is logged before-and-after, and it shows retroactively wherever attribution is displayed. |
| **Change role** | Owner-only wherever Manager or Owner is the role before or after (decision 30): an Owner promotes an Employee to Manager or Owner, demotes a Manager or an Owner, including themselves — except where that would leave the Organization with no active Owner, which is refused (decision 26). Takes effect on the next session, not retroactively. |
| **Deactivate** | Removes access **immediately at the write path** — no new work can be attributed to them from the instant it commits, each terminal's shell drops the session at its next actor re-resolution (decisions 15 and 18), and a personal session they hold ends ([E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d30). An Open Sale stays finishable. Their initials are **released** for a new hire (decision 16). Refused where it would leave the Organization with no active Owner (decision 26). A Manager deactivates Employees at their own Stores; a Manager or Owner is deactivated by an Owner (decision 30). **Users are never hard-deleted** — every Sale, adjustment, and authorization stays attributed to the person who performed it, so the record has to survive their leaving. |

## Requirements

- Attribution is permanent. A deactivated User's name still resolves on historical records.
- A manager-only authorization records the authorizing Manager separately from the acting Employee.
- A review flag records the acting Employee and the figures that raised it, and commits with the action it describes.
- The count of unreviewed flags is visible in the application shell. A review queue nobody opens is worse than a gate, because it looks like oversight.
- Role changes take effect on the next session, not retroactively on past attributions. **Deactivation does not** — it is immediate (decision 15).
- A User belongs to an Organization and is assigned to one or more of its Stores; Employees and Managers to at least one, Owners to any number including none (decision 27).
- An Organization always has at least one active Owner (decision 26). No Store is required to have a Manager assigned.
- Adding, re-roling, deactivating, reactivating and assigning a User, and setting a PIN, are manager-only ([architecture](../architecture.md) A-55); wherever the User touched is a Manager or an Owner, or the change would make them one, it is Owner-only (decision 30).
- No administrator ever types another person's password; PINs are set by an Owner or Manager and logged as changed, never as a value (decisions 28, 29).
- A deactivated User can be attributed no new work from the instant the deactivation commits; the screen follows within one actor re-resolution (decision 18).
- Every role change and activation change is logged with actor, authorizing Manager, timestamp, and the values before and after ([architecture](../architecture.md) A-55).
- Initials are unique among the **active** Users **assigned to** a Store (decisions 13, 16 and 27); PINs are unique among the Managers and Owners assigned to a Store ([E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d26).
- **Wherever initials are shown for audit, the display resolves to the User's name** — the review queue, adjustment history, a Sale's header, and both names on a manager-only authorization. Required, not cosmetic: initials are reusable, so they no longer identify a person on their own (decision 16).

## Inherited from other flows

**From [E-02](E-02-receive-inventory.md):**

- Below-cost pricing and Invoice adjustments beyond ±2% raise review flags (d35); voiding a finalized Invoice remains manager-only.
- Employees can create Suppliers but never set margins.

**From [E-01](E-01-authenticate.md):**

- A manager-only authorization is performed at a terminal with an active Employee session, without displacing it — by PIN on a store session, and not at all on a personal session ([E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d26, d27).
- **Initials are unique within a Store among the Users assigned to it, enforced when the User is created and when they are assigned** ([E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d14 as amended by d25). Add and Assign are where that rule is enforced, so it is M-04's to implement and E-01's to have decided.
- **Adjusting on hand and voiding prompt for the acting Employee's initials even inside an active session** ([E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d12, d15). Both are manager-only, so on a store session both take the Employee's initials and the authorizing Manager's PIN; on a personal session neither asks.
- **A terminal signs in with the Store's store account** ([E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d24); the store account itself is [M-06](M-06-settings.md) d70's and an Owner's. Nothing in this flow issues or resets it.
- **The optional barrier password is retired** ([E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d29), which is why decision 23 is superseded rather than extended.

**From [architecture](../architecture.md):**

- **A ReviewFlag may be raised by the system, not only by an Employee** ([architecture](../architecture.md) A-68). `actor_user_id` is **nullable** and a null actor means the system raised it — so the review queue this flow owns now renders rows with **no actor to name**, the first being `journal_imbalance` ([M-07](M-07-chart-of-accounts.md) d10). Decision 17 is unchanged: acknowledging stays manager-only and a flag is still never deleted. What changes is that the queue stops meaning *what the staff did* and starts meaning *what wants a look*, and an acknowledgement can mean *I have seen this bug*.

## Open questions

- ~~**Is there a tier above Manager?**~~ — ~~**Resolved: no.**~~ **Re-resolved: yes — the Owner (decision 25).** Decisions 11 and 14 answered it with rules for a single Store; the Organization model reopened it and decision 25 answers it with a role, decision 26 moving the floor up to that role. The [lexicon](../lexicon.md)'s *Manager* entry is updated again to match.
- ~~**Credentials** — **deferred to a later version**, not open.~~ — **Resolved** by [E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d24–d30 and decisions 28, 29 here: PIN and invite; E-01 d30 discharges the checklist this flow was on.
- ~~**Multi-store membership** — **deferred to a later version**, not open.~~ — **Resolved** by decision 27: a User is assigned to one or more Stores; decision 13 is amended, decision 14 superseded by decision 26, [E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d8 superseded by d25.
- **Authorization at a distance.** A Manager must be physically at the terminal for a manager-only action on a store session. A remote or asynchronous approval is a plausible later need in a shop where the Manager isn't always on the floor. Decision 8 shrinks this problem considerably — most of what used to need a Manager present now proceeds and flags — and [E-01](E-01-authenticate.md) d27's personal session makes a remote approval representable for the first time: a Manager on their own device is a known actor. Whether a counter action may *wait* on one is still open.
- **Must a Manager or Owner hold a PIN?** Decision 28 gives them one and says who sets it, not whether Add may save a Manager without one. A Manager with no PIN can do no manager-only work on a store session and everything on a personal session. _Recommended, not yet ratified:_ required at Add, so that the counter's manager-only line never depends on whether somebody remembered.
- **Does an Employee need an email address?** Nothing in the model uses one — no invite, no personal session, no reset (decision 29). _Recommended, not yet ratified:_ optional, kept for the day a payslip or a rota wants it.
- ~~**Audit surface**~~ — **Resolved** by decision 8: the review queue is that log.
